Sometimes the best fix for an admin problem is not software. It is a person: a freelance bookkeeper, a virtual assistant, or someone to set a tool up properly once. Hiring a freelancer can be quick and affordable, but only if you are clear about what you want.

## Step 1: Write a one-page brief

Most freelance jobs go wrong because the brief was vague. Before you post anything, write down:

- **The task:** what exactly needs doing. "Reconcile last quarter's bank transactions in Xero" beats "help with bookkeeping".
- **The outcome:** what "done" looks like.
- **The volume:** how many items, hours or pages.
- **The tools:** which software they will use.
- **The deadline:** and whether it is a one-off or ongoing.
- **Your budget:** a fixed price or an hourly range.

If you can't fill in each line, the task probably needs more thought before you hand it over.

## Step 2: Choose fixed price or hourly

- **Fixed price** works for clearly defined one-off jobs: setting up a template, cleaning up a spreadsheet, building a simple automation.
- **Hourly** suits ongoing or unpredictable work: inbox management, weekly bookkeeping, customer support.

## Step 3: Shortlist on relevant work, not just ratings

When proposals come in, look for:

1. Experience with the specific tool or task you need.
2. A reply that shows they read your brief, not a copy-and-paste pitch.
3. Clear questions about anything unclear. Good freelancers ask before they start.

## Step 4: Start with a small paid trial

Never start with the whole job. Pay for a small, real piece of it, for example ten transactions instead of three hundred. It shows you how they communicate, how accurate they are, and whether they meet deadlines. Pay fairly for the trial.

## Step 5: Share access safely

- **Give access, not passwords.** Most tools let you invite a user with limited permissions. Use that.
- **Use a password manager** if you must share a login, so you can revoke it later.
- **Limit what they can see.** Only the folders, accounts or inboxes the task needs.
- **Remove access** as soon as the work is finished.

## Step 6: Agree how you will check in

Set one simple routine: a short written update every Friday, or a ten-minute call each week. Clear updates prevent most problems.

## Using AI to speed up hiring

An AI assistant can help you write the brief. Describe the task in your own words and ask it to "turn this into a clear one-page freelance brief with scope, deliverables, deadline and questions a freelancer might ask". Edit the result and post it.

## The short version

- One-page brief: task, outcome, volume, tools, deadline, budget.
- Small paid trial first.
- Share access with limited permissions, then remove it.
- Weekly check-in.
