Do sole traders need a business bank account? In the UK, no: the law does not require one. But that is not the whole answer. Your personal bank's terms, Making Tax Digital and how much time you lose at tax time can all make a separate account the sensible choice. This guide covers the UK rules first; in the US, Australia and elsewhere the details differ, but the practical reasons for a separate account are the same, so check your local tax authority.

## Do sole traders need a business bank account? The UK rule

As a sole trader, you and the business are legally the same person. The UK government's business support site says it plainly: sole traders and ordinary partnerships "are not legally required to have business bank accounts", although separating personal and business money "makes accounting simpler".

So you can run your business through a personal current account. Many people do when they start. The question is how long that keeps working.

### Limited companies are different

A limited company is a separate legal entity from you. Its money is not your money, and the same government page lists limited companies among the businesses that must have a business bank account. If you are thinking of becoming a company later, plan for an account in the company's name.

## Can I use my personal bank account for my business?

Legally, yes. In practice, check three things first.

1. **Your bank's terms.** Many personal accounts say they are not for business use. If your bank notices regular business payments, it may ask you to move to a business account. Read your account terms, or ask your bank directly.
2. **How mixed your spending is.** If client payments, supplier bills, groceries and subscriptions all sit in one account, you will be sorting them line by line every year. Our guide to [using AI to sort a year of business expenses](/guides/use-ai-to-categorize-business-expenses/) can speed that up, but a separate account avoids most of the job.
3. **How you will keep records.** HMRC expects your records to show your business income and expenses. Keeping them apart from personal spending makes that far easier to prove.

## How Making Tax Digital changes the answer

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax means many sole traders and landlords must keep digital records and send HMRC quarterly updates through compatible software. According to GOV.UK, you need to use it when your qualifying income is over:

- **£50,000** (based on 2024 to 2025 income): from 6 April 2026
- **£30,000** (based on 2025 to 2026 income): from 6 April 2027
- **£20,000** (based on 2026 to 2027 income): from 6 April 2028

Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property **before expenses**, in other words your turnover, not your profit. That catches more people than they expect.

The rules do not say you must have a business bank account. But Making Tax Digital software works best when it pulls in a bank feed, and a feed full of personal transactions means more sorting every quarter instead of once a year. If you are near one of these thresholds, a separate account is the simplest preparation you can make. Check GOV.UK for your own dates, as thresholds and timings can change.

## What to compare before you open one

Monthly fees are only part of the cost. Compare:

1. **Transaction fees.** Some accounts charge for each payment in or out, which adds up fast.
2. **Cash deposits.** If you take cash, check where and how you can pay it in, and what it costs.
3. **Card payments.** If customers pay by card, see which card readers and payment links pay out to the account. Our guide on [card readers or payment links](/guides/card-reader-or-payment-links/) explains the choice.
4. **Accounting software links.** Make sure the account connects to the software you, your accountant or Making Tax Digital will use.
5. **Receipts and invoicing.** Some accounts let you snap receipts and send invoices from the app, which helps you [end the month-end receipt hunt](/guides/end-the-month-end-receipt-hunt/).
6. **Extra cards.** Useful if anyone else spends money for the business.
7. **Protection of deposits.** See the next section.
8. **Customer support.** When a payment is stuck, you want a real person to help.

### How your money is protected

From 1 December 2025, the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) protects up to £120,000 per eligible person at each authorised bank, building society or credit union. One detail matters for sole traders: the FSCS says a sole trader's business and personal accounts at the same bank are added together, with £120,000 covering the total. A limited company's account is protected separately from its director's personal account.

Some app-based providers are not banks and hold your money under different arrangements. Before you open an account, ask the provider whether your balance is covered by the FSCS.

## How to switch to a business account

Opening one usually takes minutes to a few days with an app-based provider. You will normally need ID, proof of your home address, your business name and start date, and a short description of what the business does. Then:

1. Ask customers to pay into the new account, and update the details on your invoices and quote templates.
2. Move direct debits for business costs, such as software, insurance and phone, to the new account.
3. Connect the account to your bookkeeping or Making Tax Digital software.
4. Pay yourself by transferring money to your personal account, rather than spending business money on personal things.
5. Keep the personal account for personal life only.

## FAQ

### Is it illegal for a sole trader to use a personal bank account?

No. In the UK there is no legal requirement for a sole trader to have a business bank account. Check your bank's terms, though, because many personal accounts are not meant for business use.

### Does HMRC check your personal bank account?

HMRC can ask to see records that support your tax return, and if business money runs through a personal account, that account is part of those records. A separate business account keeps business and personal transactions apart, which makes your records clearer if questions come up.

### Do I need a business bank account for Making Tax Digital?

The Making Tax Digital rules require digital records and quarterly updates, not a business bank account. A dedicated account does make those records much quicker to keep, because the software sees only business transactions.

## The short version

- Sole traders in the UK do not legally need a business bank account. Limited companies do.
- Your personal bank's terms may still rule out business use, so check them.
- Making Tax Digital starts at £50,000 of turnover from April 2026, £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028. A separate account makes it much easier.
- Compare fees, cash deposits, software links and FSCS protection before you choose.
- For sole traders, FSCS protection covers business and personal money at the same bank together, up to £120,000.
