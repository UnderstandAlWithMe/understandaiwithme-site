When a small business hires its first few people, joining and leaving is handled from memory. Someone sets up an email address, someone else adds them to payroll, and a laptop gets handed over. It works until the team grows and the tools multiply. Then things slip: a new starter waits days for a login, or a leaver can still get into a shared drive months later.

A single checklist fixes most of this. It also shows you, very clearly, whether your tools have become a problem.

## Step 1: List every tool a person touches

Sit down with whoever handles people and IT (often that is you) and list every system a typical team member uses. Group them like this:

- **Identity and email:** email account, password manager, any single sign-on.
- **Work tools:** chat, file storage, project management, CRM, accounting or booking software.
- **Money and people:** payroll, HR records, leave tracking, expenses, benefits.
- **Devices and physical access:** laptop, phone, keys, alarm codes, door fobs.
- **Customer-facing accounts:** social media, website admin, supplier portals, marketplaces.

Next to each, write who sets it up and who can remove access. Gaps in that second column are your biggest risk.

## Step 2: Build the starter checklist

Turn your list into steps, in the order they need to happen.

### Before day one

1. Collect the details you need for payroll and HR records, using your local official guidance on what to collect.
2. Create the email account and add the person to the password manager.
3. Prepare the laptop or phone and note its serial number against their name.
4. Add them to the tools their role needs, and only those.

### First week

1. Walk them through where files live and how to ask for access to more.
2. Turn on two-step verification for their key accounts.
3. Confirm they appear correctly in payroll and leave tracking before the first pay run.

## Step 3: Build the leaver checklist

Leavers are where small businesses most often get caught out, because access is easy to forget once someone has gone.

1. **Agree the last working day** and tell whoever runs payroll, so final pay is handled correctly.
2. **On the last day, remove access** to email, chat, file storage and every tool on your list. Change any shared passwords or codes they knew.
3. **Transfer ownership** of files, calendars and accounts registered in their name, such as a social media account or supplier portal.
4. **Set up email forwarding or an auto-reply** for a period, so customers are not left hanging.
5. **Collect devices, keys and cards,** and tick them off against your records.
6. **Keep the records you are required to keep.** Employment and tax record rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority or government business site.

Removing access is not about distrust. Most people leave on good terms. It simply protects them as well as you if an old account is later misused.

## Step 4: Use AI to draft and maintain it

An AI assistant such as ChatGPT or Claude can turn your rough list into a clean checklist in a minute. Try:

"Here is a list of the tools and accounts our team uses, with who manages each one. Turn it into two checklists, one for new starters and one for leavers, in the order steps should happen. Add a column for who is responsible. Flag any tool with no one responsible for removing access."

Paste tool names and roles only, not anyone's personal details. Then save the checklist somewhere everyone can find it, and update it whenever you add a new tool.

## Step 5: Time one starter and one leaver

Next time someone joins or leaves, note how long the checklist takes and how many separate systems you had to log into. That number tells you whether you have a tools problem.

### Signs you have outgrown separate tools

- The same details (name, address, start date, job title) are typed into three or more systems.
- Nobody is sure which tools a leaver still has access to.
- Device and software setup for a new starter takes more than a day.
- Payroll mistakes trace back to changes that were not passed on.

If several of these sound familiar, it may be time to look at one connected HR, payroll and IT platform. When you speak to vendors, ask them to demo a full starter-to-leaver journey using your checklist, not their standard sales demo.

## The short version

- List every tool and account a team member touches, and who can remove access.
- Build one starter checklist and one leaver checklist, in order.
- On a leaver's last day, remove access and change shared passwords.
- Check local rules on what employment records to keep.
- Time the process. If it touches too many systems, consider one connected platform.
