If customers regularly ask "can I pay by card?" and the answer is no, you are probably losing some sales and spending time chasing cash or bank transfers. The good news is that taking card payments is now simple for even the smallest business. The main decision is *how* you take them.

*We are not financial advisers. Always check a provider's current fees, contract terms and protections before signing up.*

## The two main options

### Card readers

A card reader is a small device that takes tap, chip and phone payments in person. Many connect to a free app on your phone or tablet. Some phones can also take tap payments directly with no extra hardware, depending on your provider and country.

A reader suits you if you get paid **face to face**: market stalls, cafes, salons, mobile trades, events and shops.

### Payment links and online invoices

A payment link is a web link you send by text, email or messaging app. The customer taps it and pays by card on their own phone. Many invoicing and accounting tools can add a "pay now" button to every invoice.

Links suit you if you get paid **after the work is done or at a distance**: tradespeople who invoice after the job, consultants, tutors, freelancers and anyone taking deposits.

Many businesses end up using both, so it is worth choosing a provider that offers both from one account.

## Step 1: Work out how you actually get paid

Before comparing providers, answer three questions:

1. Roughly what share of your payments happen in person, and what share after the job?
2. What is your typical sale size: small and frequent, or large and occasional?
3. Do you need to take deposits or payments before the work starts?

Your answers tell you whether to start with a reader, links or both.

## Step 2: Compare fees on your own numbers

Card fees are usually charged as a percentage of each sale, sometimes with a small fixed fee on top. Some providers also charge a monthly subscription, or a different rate for online payments than in-person ones.

The cheapest headline rate is not always cheapest for you. To compare fairly:

1. Take a typical month of your sales: number of payments and total value.
2. Work out the cost with each provider's published fees, including any monthly fee and the cost of the reader.
3. Compare the total, not just the percentage.

A small fixed fee per payment matters more if you take lots of small payments. A monthly subscription only makes sense if your volume is high enough for its lower rate to pay off.

## Step 3: Check payout times and contracts

Ask each provider:

- **How quickly does money reach my bank account?** Next working day is common, but it varies.
- **Is there a contract or minimum term?** Many small-business options are pay-as-you-go.
- **Can I cancel easily, and what happens to the reader?**
- **Does it connect to my accounting software?** Automatic syncing saves reconciliation time later.

## Step 4: Set it up properly from day one

1. Add your business name exactly as customers know it, so it shows clearly on their bank statement. Unfamiliar names lead to confused customers and disputes.
2. Turn on digital receipts by email or text.
3. Add a payment link or "pay now" button to your invoice template.
4. Put a small "we take cards" sign at your counter or stall, and say it on your quotes.

## Step 5: Keep card data safe

Let the reader or the payment page handle the card details. Never write card numbers down, store them in a spreadsheet or ask customers to send them by email or message. The PCI Security Standards Council, which sets the global card security rules, advises small merchants that if you do not need card data, you should not store it. Using a reputable provider's reader or hosted payment page keeps the card details off your systems.

Also check your provider's guidance on chargebacks (when a customer disputes a payment) so you know what records to keep, such as receipts, signed quotes or proof of delivery.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing on headline rate alone.** Monthly fees and fixed per-payment fees change the picture.
- **Forgetting about after-the-job payments.** A reader does not help if most customers pay later. Links do.
- **Taking card details over the phone and writing them down.** Use a payment link instead.

## The short version

- In person? Start with a card reader. Paid after the job? Start with payment links.
- Compare providers on a real month of your sales, including all fees.
- Check payout speed, contract terms and accounting software sync.
- Add payment links to every invoice and turn on digital receipts.
- Never store card numbers yourself.
