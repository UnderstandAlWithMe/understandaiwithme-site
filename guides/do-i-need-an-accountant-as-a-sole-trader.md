"Do I need an accountant as a sole trader, or can I do my own tax return?" The question gets louder as the 31 January Self Assessment deadline gets closer. The honest answer: no UK rule says you must have one, and many sole traders with simple books file their own return. This guide gives you a five-minute check to see which side you are on, the complete free route if you do it yourself, and what to look for if you get help.

## Do I need an accountant as a sole trader? The UK rule

No. You can register, keep your records and send your Self Assessment return online yourself. GOV.UK lists an accountant as one option for filling in and sending your return, alongside a friend or relative, and explains how to [appoint someone to deal with HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/appoint-tax-agent) if you want to.

What you cannot skip are the deadlines (GOV.UK, checked 28 November 2026):

1. **Register by 5 October** after the end of the tax year you started trading (tax years run 6 April to 5 April). You must register as a sole trader if you earn more than £1,000 from self-employment in a tax year.
2. **File online by 31 January.** For the 2025 to 2026 tax year, that is 31 January 2027.
3. **Pay what you owe by 31 January** too.

Filing late starts with a £100 penalty, then £10 a day after three months (up to £900), with more at six and twelve months. Paying late adds 5% penalties and interest. See the [Self Assessment penalties page](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/penalties).

## When doing it yourself is usually fine

- You run one simple business with a handful of regular costs.
- Business money is easy to pick out, ideally in its own account. Our guide on [whether sole traders need a business bank account](/guides/do-sole-traders-need-a-business-bank-account/) explains why that helps.
- You are not VAT registered and not in the Construction Industry Scheme (CIS).
- Your records are up to date, so you are copying totals, not rebuilding a year.

## When an accountant is usually worth it

1. **You are VAT registered or close to it.** VAT returns add a second set of deadlines and rules.
2. **You work under CIS**, where contractors take deductions from your pay that you need to track.
3. **Your turnover is heading towards Making Tax Digital.** GOV.UK says it applies when qualifying income is over £50,000 from 6 April 2026, £30,000 from 6 April 2027 and £20,000 from 6 April 2028 (checked 24 November 2026). Qualifying income means turnover before expenses, and it brings quarterly updates through software.
4. **Business and personal money are mixed up**, and sorting them would take days.
5. **You are behind**, or have a letter from HMRC.
6. **You have several income sources**, or are thinking of becoming a limited company.

## The 5-minute check

Answer yes or no:

1. Am I VAT registered, or in CIS?
2. Is my turnover over £30,000, or likely to pass it soon?
3. Would sorting my business transactions take more than a day?
4. Am I behind on a return or records?
5. Do I have more than one source of income to declare?
6. Would I rather spend those hours on paid work?

**No to all six:** try the free route below. **Yes to one or two:** the free route can still work, but price up help before January. **Yes to three or more:** an accountant is likely to be worth the fee.

## The free route: file your own return

1. **Register and get your Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR)** from HMRC. You need a National Insurance number to register.
2. **Collect the year's money in and out** from your bank, card reader and payment apps.
3. **Sort costs into categories.** AI can do the first pass in minutes: see [using AI to categorise business expenses](/guides/use-ai-to-categorize-business-expenses/) for the prompt and the checks.
4. **Match receipts to the bigger costs.** If receipts are always the bottleneck, see how to [end the month-end receipt hunt](/guides/end-the-month-end-receipt-hunt/).
5. **Fill in the return in your HMRC online account**, one section at a time. GOV.UK suggests checking everything before you submit; you can change an online return afterwards if you spot a mistake.
6. **Pay by 31 January**, and set a reminder to keep next year's records monthly.

### Can ChatGPT do my accounts?

Not on its own. AI is good at sorting a bank export and explaining terms in plain English. It does not file with HMRC, it can be confidently wrong, and the numbers on your return are yours. Use it to speed up steps 2 to 4, then check every figure.

## FAQ

### Can I do my own tax return as a sole trader?

Yes. Register with HMRC, keep records of income and costs, and file online by 31 January after the end of the tax year.

### Is an accountant worth it for Self Assessment?

For one simple business with tidy records, often not. With VAT, CIS, turnover near the Making Tax Digital thresholds or late records, the fee often costs less than your time and the risk of mistakes.

### When should I get an accountant before the deadline?

As early as you can. Accountants get busy in December and January and need your records before they start, so look in November or early December.
