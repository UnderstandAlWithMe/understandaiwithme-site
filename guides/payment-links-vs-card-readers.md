If customers regularly say "can I pay by card?" and the answer is "only by bank transfer", you are probably losing time, and sometimes the sale. The good news is that taking card payments no longer means a long contract or expensive terminal. For most small businesses, the choice comes down to a payment link, a card reader, or both.

This guide explains the difference, helps you decide which you need, and lists what to check before you sign up. It applies wherever you are based, though providers, fees and features vary by country.

## Payment links: getting paid without being there

A payment link is a web address that opens a secure checkout page. You send it by email, text message or chat, and the customer pays on their own phone or computer. Many invoicing and accounting tools can add a "pay now" link to every invoice automatically.

Payment links suit you if:

- You finish the job and invoice afterward, as many trades, consultants and freelancers do.
- You take deposits before booking someone in.
- Customers pay remotely, for example for online sessions or orders by phone.
- You rarely meet customers face to face at the moment they pay.

The main benefit is speed. A customer who can pay with two taps from the invoice email is more likely to pay that day than one who has to log in to their banking app and type in your details.

## Card readers: getting paid on the spot

A card reader is a small device that takes contactless, chip and mobile wallet payments in person. Most connect to an app on your phone or tablet. Some phones can also accept contactless payments directly through an app, with no extra hardware, depending on the provider and your country.

Card readers suit you if:

- You sell in person: market stalls, cafes, salons, events, shops.
- You want payment before the customer leaves.
- Customers expect to tap a card or phone, not wait for an invoice.

## Many businesses need both

A mobile hairdresser might take payment by reader after each appointment and send payment links for deposits. A builder might send a link with each stage invoice and use a reader for small jobs paid on completion. Many providers offer both from the same account, which keeps all your payments and reports in one place.

## Five questions to ask before you choose a provider

### 1. What does each payment really cost?

Look at the fee per transaction, not only the monthly price. Fees are usually a percentage of the sale, sometimes with a small fixed amount on top, and they can differ for in-person payments, online payments, payment links and cards from other countries. Work out the cost on your average sale. A fee that looks small matters a lot on low-value sales, while a monthly plan with lower transaction fees may suit higher volumes.

### 2. How quickly does the money reach your bank account?

Payout times range from the same day to several working days. If cash flow is tight, faster payouts can matter more than a slightly lower fee. Check whether faster payouts cost extra.

### 3. Does it connect to your accounting software?

If payments sync to your bookkeeping automatically, you save the time spent matching them later. Check that the provider connects with the software you already use, and what details come across, such as fees and refunds.

### 4. Is there a contract or monthly minimum?

Many small-business providers have no long contract, but some do. Check for monthly fees, minimum spend, early exit fees and what the card reader itself costs to buy or rent.

### 5. What happens when something goes wrong?

Ask how refunds work, how disputes and chargebacks are handled, and how you reach support. If you sell at weekends or evenings, check whether support is available then.

## How to set it up in an afternoon

1. **Pick one provider** based on the five questions above. Do not open accounts with several at once.
2. **Complete the verification steps.** Providers usually need your business details and identity documents before they pay out.
3. **Connect it to your accounting software** if you use one.
4. **Add a pay-now link to your invoice template** so every invoice has one.
5. **Order a reader** only if you take in-person payments.
6. **Do a test payment** with your own card, then refund it, so you know both steps work.
7. **Tell customers.** Add "We accept card payments" to your website, quotes and invoices.

## Staying safe

- Never ask customers to read out or email their full card details. A payment link or reader keeps those details with the provider.
- Use a strong password and two-step verification on your payment account.
- Be wary of "overpayments" where someone pays too much and asks you to refund the difference to a different account.

## The short version

- **Payment links** suit businesses that invoice after the work or take deposits.
- **Card readers** suit businesses that are paid face to face.
- Many businesses benefit from both, ideally from one provider.
- Compare the real cost on your average sale, payout speed, accounting connections, contracts and support.
- Add a pay-now link to every invoice and run a test payment before you go live.
