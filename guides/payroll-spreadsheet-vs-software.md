*This guide covers UK payroll. The same spreadsheet-versus-software logic applies in the US and Australia, but the reporting rules differ.*

When you take on your first employee, payroll can look like a simple sum: hours times rate, minus tax. In practice, it is a set of legal reporting jobs that repeat every pay day. That is why it so often ends up eating a whole day a month.

## What payroll involves in the UK

Whatever tool you use, an employer generally needs to:

- **Register as an employer** with HMRC before the first pay day.
- **Calculate pay and deductions**, including Income Tax, National Insurance, student loans and pension contributions.
- **Report to HMRC on or before each pay day** through Real Time Information (RTI), using payroll software that can submit to HMRC.
- **Give each employee a payslip.**
- **Handle workplace pension auto-enrolment** for eligible staff.
- **Keep payroll records**, which HMRC expects you to keep for several years.

GOV.UK has a step-by-step guide to running payroll that covers each of these. HMRC also offers free payroll software (Basic PAYE Tools) for employers with fewer than 10 employees.

## Why spreadsheets break down

A spreadsheet can calculate pay. The trouble is everything around it:

1. **You cannot report to HMRC from a spreadsheet.** You still need payroll software for RTI submissions.
2. **Data lives in several places.** Holiday requests in email, contracts in a folder, hours in a chat. Each copy is a chance for mistakes.
3. **Changes are manual.** New tax codes, a pay rise, a leaver, statutory sick pay: each one means editing formulas by hand.
4. **It depends on one person.** If the person who understands the spreadsheet is off sick, payroll stops.

## When to switch to software

Software is usually worth it when any of these is true:

- You have more than two or three employees.
- People work variable hours or shifts.
- You spend more than an hour or two on each pay run.
- You also manage holiday, rotas or onboarding in separate places.

## What to look for

- **HMRC-recognised payroll** that handles RTI submissions automatically.
- **Pension integration** so contributions flow to your provider without re-keying.
- **Employee self-service:** staff see payslips and request leave themselves.
- **HR basics in the same system:** contracts, documents, holiday and onboarding.
- **Accounting integration** so payroll journals reach your books automatically.

## How to test a tool properly

Do not judge payroll software on a generic demo. Before you sign up:

1. Write down one real pay run: the people, the hours, anyone joining or leaving, any sick pay.
2. Ask the provider to walk through exactly that scenario.
3. Ask how they handle corrections after a submission has been sent.
4. Check what support you get, and how fast, on pay day.

## The short version

Payroll is a reporting job as much as a maths job. If you are re-keying data between a spreadsheet, HMRC tools and email every month, an all-in-one payroll and HR platform will usually give you that day back.
