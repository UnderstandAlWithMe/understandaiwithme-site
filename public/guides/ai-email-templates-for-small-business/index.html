Use AI to Write Customer Emails in Your Own Voice: A 20-Minute Setup

By Constantin · 2026-10-28 · 3 min read

Most small businesses send the same dozen emails over and over: quotes, follow-ups, booking confirmations, "thanks for your enquiry", gentle nudges. Writing each one from scratch is a quiet time sink. AI can draft them in seconds, but only if you set it up properly once.

This guide takes about 20 minutes and works with ChatGPT, Claude or similar assistants.

Step 1: Collect five of your best emails

Find five emails you have sent that you are happy with. Pick a mix: a quote, a follow-up, a reply to a complaint, a booking confirmation, a thank-you. Remove customer names, addresses and anything private.

Step 2: Write a short "how we write" note

Answer these in a few lines each:

  • Who are your customers? For example: homeowners in Leeds who want a kitchen fitted.
  • How do you sound? Friendly and direct? Formal? Do you use first names?
  • What do you always include? Prices with VAT, a call to action, your phone number.
  • What do you never say? Jargon, pushy sales lines, promises about dates you cannot keep.

Step 3: Save it as a project or custom instructions

Most AI assistants let you save standing instructions so you do not repeat yourself:

  • In Claude, create a Project and paste your note and example emails into the project instructions or knowledge.
  • In ChatGPT, use a custom GPT or a project with your note and examples.

Add one line at the end: "When I give you a situation, draft an email in this style. Keep it under 150 words unless I say otherwise."

Step 4: Draft emails from short prompts

Now each email starts with one line from you:

  • "Quote follow-up. Sent a quote for a bathroom refit 5 days ago, no reply."
  • "Reply to a customer who says the delivery was late. Apologise, explain the supplier delay, offer 10% off the next order."
  • "Confirm a booking for Tuesday at 10am, remind them to clear the driveway."

You get a draft in your voice. Edit, then send.

Step 5: Keep it safe

AI assistants are useful, but treat them like any outside service:

  • Do not paste sensitive personal data such as bank details, health information or passwords.
  • Check facts before sending. AI can confidently invent prices, dates or policies. Make sure numbers come from you.
  • Check your plan's data settings. Business plans often have stronger privacy controls. Read the provider's settings on whether your conversations are used for training.
  • You are responsible for what you send. Always read the draft.

Step 6: Turn the best drafts into templates

When a draft works well, save it as a template in your email tool. Over a month you will build a library that covers most of your everyday emails, and the AI handles the unusual ones.

When to go further

If you send dozens of similar emails a day, or the information already sits in a system such as a CRM, booking tool or spreadsheet, a small custom automation can draft or even send them for you. That is where a done-for-you build starts to pay off.

The short version

  • Five good examples plus a short tone note.
  • Saved once as a project or custom instructions.
  • One-line prompts, human review, then send.
