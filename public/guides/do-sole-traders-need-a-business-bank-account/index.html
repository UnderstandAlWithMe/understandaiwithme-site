Do Sole Traders Need a Business Bank Account? (UK Guide)

By Constantin · 2026-10-27 · 2 min read

It is one of the most common questions from people starting out, and the answer depends on how your business is set up. This guide covers the UK rules; in the US, Australia and elsewhere the details differ, but the practical reasons for a separate account are the same.

Limited companies: yes

A limited company is a separate legal entity from you. Its money is not your money, so in practice it needs a bank account in the company's name. Mixing company and personal money causes problems with your accounts and tax.

Sole traders: not legally required, but usually worth it

As a sole trader, you and the business are legally the same person, so there is no legal requirement for a separate business account. You can run your business through a personal account.

There are good reasons not to, though:

  • Your bank may not allow it. Many personal accounts have terms that say they are not for business use. Check yours.
  • Bookkeeping is much easier. When every transaction in the account is a business one, you are not separating the coffee from the client payments at tax time.
  • Tax returns are quicker and cleaner. You can see your business income and expenses at a glance.
  • You look more professional. Customers pay a business name, not a personal one.
  • Digital record keeping is coming. HMRC is rolling out Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, which requires many sole traders and landlords above certain income levels to keep digital records and send quarterly updates. A dedicated account linked to software makes that much easier. Check GOV.UK for the current thresholds and dates.

What to compare before you open one

Monthly fees are only part of the cost. Compare:

  1. Transaction fees. Some accounts charge for each payment in or out, which adds up fast.
  2. Cash deposits. If you take cash, check where and how you can pay it in, and what it costs.
  3. Invoicing and receipts. Some accounts include invoicing, receipt capture and tax estimates.
  4. Accounting software links. Make sure the account connects to the software you or your accountant use.
  5. Extra cards. Useful if anyone else spends money for the business.
  6. Protection of deposits. Check whether your money is covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) or held under different arrangements, especially with newer providers.
  7. Customer support. When a payment is stuck, you want a real person to help.

Getting set up

Opening a business account usually takes minutes to a few days with an app-based provider. You will normally need ID, your home address and basic details about what the business does. Once it is open:

  • Move every business income and payment to the new account.
  • Connect it to your accounting software.
  • Pay yourself by transferring money to your personal account, rather than spending from the business account on personal things.

The short version

Limited companies need a business account. Sole traders do not legally need one, but it usually saves time, avoids issues with your bank's terms, and makes tax and digital record keeping far simpler.

We are not financial advisers. Always check a provider's terms, fees and protections before opening an account.
