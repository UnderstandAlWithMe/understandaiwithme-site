Can I Do Payroll on a Spreadsheet? A Plain Answer for Small UK Businesses

By Constantin · 2026-10-25 · 6 min read

Can I do payroll on a spreadsheet? In the UK, partly. A spreadsheet can work out hours and pay, but it cannot send the report HMRC needs every time you pay someone, so you will always need payroll software alongside it. The good news is that small employers can use free software from HMRC. This guide explains what payroll involves in the UK, where a spreadsheet still helps, and the signs it is time to move everything into one system. We are not financial advisers; check anything that affects your tax with HMRC or an accountant.

This guide covers UK payroll. The same spreadsheet-versus-software logic applies in the US and Australia, but the reporting rules differ, so check your local tax authority (the IRS in the US, the ATO in Australia, the CRA in Canada).

What payroll involves in the UK

When you take on your first employee, payroll can look like a simple sum: hours times rate, minus tax. In practice, it is a set of reporting jobs that repeat every pay day. GOV.UK sets them out, and an employer generally needs to:

  • Register as an employer with HMRC. GOV.UK says you must register before the first pay day, and you cannot register more than 2 months before you start paying people. You also need to register if you only employ yourself, for example as the only director of a limited company.
  • Work out pay and deductions, including Income Tax, National Insurance, student loans and pension contributions.
  • Report to HMRC on or before each pay day. Every time you pay staff, you send a Full Payment Submission (FPS: the report of what you paid and deducted) using payroll software.
  • Give each employee a payslip.
  • Handle workplace pension auto-enrolment for eligible staff (The Pensions Regulator explains your duties).
  • Keep payroll records for 3 years from the end of the tax year they relate to.

(Checked on GOV.UK, 26 Nov 2026.)

Can I do payroll on a spreadsheet? What it can and can't do

A spreadsheet is fine for the parts that are just arithmetic and lists:

  • Adding up hours from timesheets.
  • Keeping a simple list of who is paid what, and when.
  • Planning: "what would a second hire cost us each month?"

What it can't do is the legal part. There is no way to send an FPS from a spreadsheet, so you would still need payroll software to report each pay run. That means typing the same numbers twice, once into the sheet and once into the software, every pay day.

The free option: HMRC's Basic PAYE Tools

GOV.UK describes Basic PAYE Tools as free payroll software from HMRC for businesses with fewer than 10 employees. You need to be registered as an employer and have a PAYE Online login. It works on Windows, Mac and Linux. HMRC also lists other free payroll software it recognises for employers with fewer than 10 staff, and says it cannot recommend one product over another.

So for a very small, simple team, the cheapest honest setup is: one free piece of recognised payroll software, with a spreadsheet only for collecting hours, if you need one at all.

Why spreadsheets break down

Even with free software for the reporting, a spreadsheet-led payroll causes problems as you grow:

  1. Double entry. Every pay run means copying figures from the sheet into the payroll software. Each copy is a chance for a typo.
  2. Data lives in several places. Holiday requests in email, contracts in a folder, hours in a chat. If that sounds familiar, our guide to stopping the timesheet chase before payroll fixes the hours part first.
  3. Changes are manual. New tax codes, a pay rise, a leaver, statutory sick pay: each one means editing formulas by hand.
  4. It depends on one person. If the person who understands the spreadsheet is off sick on pay day, payroll stops.
  5. Records get messy. HMRC expects you to keep pay, deductions, reports, payments, tax code notices and absence records. GOV.UK warns that if you don't keep full records, HMRC may estimate what you owe and charge a penalty of up to £3,000.

When to switch to proper payroll software

Moving from "spreadsheet plus basic tool" to full payroll software is usually worth it when any of these is true:

  • You have more than two or three employees, or are heading towards 10.
  • People work variable hours or shifts.
  • You spend more than an hour or two on each pay run.
  • You also manage holiday, rotas, starters and leavers in separate places.

What to look for in payroll software

  • HMRC-recognised payroll that sends your FPS automatically on or before pay day.
  • Pension integration so contributions flow to your provider without re-keying.
  • Employee self-service: staff see payslips and request leave themselves.
  • HR basics in the same system: contracts, documents, holiday and onboarding. A starter and leaver checklist shows everything a person touches when they join or leave.
  • Accounting integration so payroll totals reach your books without copying. If you have an accountant, ask which payroll tools they already work with (see how to choose an accountant).

How to test a tool properly

Do not judge payroll software on a generic demo. Before you sign up:

  1. Write down one real pay run: the people, the hours, anyone joining or leaving, any sick pay.
  2. Ask the provider to walk through exactly that scenario.
  3. Ask how they handle corrections after a submission has been sent.
  4. Check what support you get, and how fast, on pay day.
  5. Ask how you get your records out if you ever leave, since you must keep them for 3 years.

FAQ

Can I run payroll without software in the UK?

You can calculate pay by hand or in a spreadsheet, but you report each pay run to HMRC through payroll software. HMRC's Basic PAYE Tools is free for businesses with fewer than 10 employees, so cost doesn't have to stop you.

Is HMRC's Basic PAYE Tools good enough for a small business?

For a handful of staff on regular pay, it covers the HMRC reporting. GOV.UK says it has some limitations and isn't designed for agents or bookkeepers. If you want payslips emailed to staff, pension uploads, holiday tracking or accounting links, a paid tool usually saves more time than it costs.

How long do I need to keep payroll records?

GOV.UK says 3 years from the end of the tax year they relate to. That includes pay and deductions, reports and payments to HMRC, tax code notices and sickness or leave records. Minimum wage and holiday pay records have separate rules, so check GOV.UK.

The short version

  • A spreadsheet can work out pay, but you can't report to HMRC from it.
  • Every pay day, you send an FPS through payroll software, on or before pay day.
  • Under 10 employees, HMRC's Basic PAYE Tools and other recognised free tools cost nothing.
  • Keep payroll records for 3 years from the end of the tax year.
  • Switch to full payroll and HR software once double entry, shifts or a growing team eat more than an hour or two each pay run.
