How to Choose an Accountant for Your Small Business: Questions to Ask and What to Check

By Constantin · 2026-11-10 · 4 min read

We are not financial advisers. This guide explains how to choose and check an accountant; it is not tax or financial advice. Rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority or government business site.

A good accountant saves you admin and keeps you on top of deadlines. A poor fit leaves you chasing answers and paying for work you thought was included. The difference usually comes down to a few questions asked before you sign up.

Step 1: Decide what you actually need

Before you contact anyone, write a short list of what you want help with. Common jobs include:

  • Annual tax returns for you and the business.
  • Bookkeeping, or checking the bookkeeping you do yourself.
  • Payroll for staff.
  • Sales tax, VAT or GST returns, if your business is registered.
  • Advice on business structure, such as whether to form a company.
  • Year-end accounts for a company.

Note which accounting software you use and roughly how many transactions you have each month. Accountants often price on these details.

Step 2: Check they are qualified or registered

Rules on who can prepare tax returns differ by country. Here is a brief overview of three.

United States

The IRS says anyone can be a paid tax return preparer as long as they have a Preparer Tax Identification Number (PTIN), and all paid preparers must have one. Preparers include certified public accountants (CPAs), enrolled agents, attorneys and others without a professional credential. The IRS publishes a Directory of Federal Tax Return Preparers listing those with credentials and select qualifications. See the IRS guide on choosing a tax professional.

United Kingdom

GOV.UK states that anyone can call themselves a tax agent and that HMRC does not regulate agents. It suggests checking whether an agent holds relevant professional qualifications or belongs to an accountancy or tax professional body, as members are more likely to hold professional indemnity insurance. See how to choose a tax agent on GOV.UK.

Australia

The Tax Practitioners Board (TPB) says that anyone preparing or lodging your tax returns or giving you tax advice should be registered with it. You can search the TPB register to confirm. Registered BAS agents can provide only some of the services a registered tax agent can, so check which one you need.

Elsewhere, look for your national tax authority's guidance on choosing a tax professional.

Step 3: Ask these questions before you sign up

  1. What exactly does the fee include? Ask about tax returns, payroll, VAT or sales tax returns, year-end accounts and how many questions you can ask during the year.
  2. What costs extra? Find out how they charge for one-off work, such as dealing with a tax authority enquiry or catching up on late bookkeeping.
  3. Do you work with my accounting software? If they prefer a different system, ask whether moving is worth it and who does the work.
  4. Who will I deal with? A named contact is easier than a general inbox, especially near deadlines.
  5. Do you work with businesses like mine? Experience in your trade or business size helps. HMRC suggests asking about this too.
  6. What do you need from me, and by when? A good accountant will give you clear deadlines for sending records.
  7. Will I get a written agreement? Many accountants send an engagement letter that sets out the work, fees and responsibilities. Read it before you sign.

Step 4: Watch for warning signs

  • Pressure to sign a return without reading it. GOV.UK says you must not sign a blank return for your agent to fill in, and the IRS advises always reviewing your business tax return before signing it.
  • Asking for your tax account login. GOV.UK says never to share your sign-in details for HMRC online services with your agent. Most tax authorities have a proper way to authorize an agent instead.
  • Promises that sound too good to be true, such as guaranteed large refunds.
  • Vague pricing, or no written terms.
  • Not available outside tax season. The IRS suggests making sure your preparer is available throughout the year.

Step 5: Get two or three quotes and compare

Send the same short brief from Step 1 to two or three accountants and compare what is included, not just the price. A cheaper plan with lots of extras can end up costing more.

Step 6: Make the handover easy

Give your accountant access to your accounting software rather than emailing spreadsheets, authorize them with your tax authority using the official process, and set calendar reminders a few weeks before each deadline.

The short version

  • List the jobs you need done and the software you use.
  • Check credentials or registration: IRS directory in the US, a professional body in the UK, the TPB register in Australia.
  • Ask what the fee includes, what costs extra and who your contact is.
  • Never sign a blank return or share your tax account login.
  • Compare two or three quotes on what is included, not just price.
  • We are not financial advisers; check your local tax authority for the rules where you are.
