A Free Quote Follow-Up System for Tradespeople (AI Writes the Emails)

By Constantin · 2026-11-22 · 6 min read

Prefer video?

You send a quote, the customer sounds keen, and then nothing. You mean to chase it, but you are on a job all day and by the evening it has slipped your mind again. A quote follow-up system fixes that. It sends a short thank-you straight away, then two polite check-ins over the next week, written in your own voice, while you get on with the work.

A quote that goes quiet is often not a no. It is a busy customer who needed one friendly nudge. This guide shows plumbers, electricians, cleaners, decorators and any other trade how to set the nudges up once, for free, without coding.

What you will build

  • A short quote request page where customers leave their name, email and what they need doing.
  • Three short emails, drafted by AI and edited by you: a thank-you on day 0, a check-in on day 3 and a last, no-pressure note on day 7.
  • One automation rule that starts the emails when someone fills in the form. An automation rule is simply an "if this happens, do that" instruction.
  • A weekly check so a person, not a machine, handles anyone who goes quiet.

You need an email tool that can send a sequence of emails on a timer. How to build a quote follow-up system in five steps

Step 1: Make a quote request page

Create a simple form page with three boxes: name, email, and "What do you need doing?" Keep the headline plain, such as "Get a quote for your plumbing job". In Systeme.io this is a funnel with an opt-in page; in other tools it may be called a form or landing page.

Put the page link where customers already find you: your Google Business Profile, website, email signature and the text you send when someone rings for a price.

If you already send quotes from job software, you can skip the page and add customers to the sequence by hand when you send each quote.

If most of your quote requests arrive by phone, our guide to turning customer calls into notes and a follow-up email makes sure the quote covers everything the customer asked for.

Step 2: Let AI write the three emails

Open ChatGPT, Claude or another AI assistant. If you have already written a short "how we write" note (see our guide to AI emails in your own voice), paste it in first. Then paste this prompt and change the details to fit your trade:

"I'm a local plumber. My customers are homeowners. I sound friendly, direct and never pushy. Write three short emails for someone who has asked for a quote. Email one, sent straight away: thank them and say when they'll hear from me. Email two, three days later: check the quote arrived and offer to answer questions. Email three, seven days later: a last, no-pressure note. Each under 100 words, ending with my phone number."

Read each draft out loud. If it sounds like something you would never say to a customer's face, ask the AI to make it "plainer" or "more like a text message". Check every fact yourself: AI can invent prices, dates or promises.

Step 3: Put the emails in order and switch them on

In your email tool, create a campaign called "Quote follow-up" and add the three emails with delays of 0, 3 and 7 days. Then add one rule: when someone fills in the quote form, add them to the "Quote follow-up" campaign.

Before you share the page, fill in the form yourself with your own email address and check that all three emails arrive, read well on a phone and show the right phone number.

Step 4: Stop the chase the moment they reply

This is the step most people miss. When a customer books, replies with a question or says no thanks, take them out of the campaign straight away. In Systeme.io, open Contacts, find the person and unsubscribe them from the campaign. On a free plan this is usually a manual step, and that is fine.

As a safety net, add one line to emails two and three: "If you've already gone ahead, please ignore this, and thank you." Without this step, the system keeps chasing someone who already said yes, which is what makes automatic emails feel robotic.

Step 5: A five-minute check every Friday

Once a week, look at your contacts. Who asked for a quote, who replied, and who went quiet after all three emails? Those last few are worth one personal phone call. The system does the remembering; you do the human part.

Write follow-ups that do not feel pushy

  • Be specific. Mention the job: "the boiler service quote I sent on Tuesday" beats "my recent quote".
  • Make replying easy. One question, one phone number, no long sign-off.
  • Never invent urgency. Do not claim a price expires or a slot is nearly gone unless it is true.
  • Keep it about their job. These emails are about the quote they asked for. Adding people to a marketing newsletter is a separate decision and usually needs their permission. Rules on marketing emails differ by country (the ICO in the UK, the FTC in the US and ACMA in Australia publish guidance), so check your local rules.

FAQ

How soon should you follow up on a quote?

A thank-you straight away, a check-in after about three days and a final note after about a week suits most small jobs. For large or expensive jobs, customers may need longer to compare quotes, so stretch the gaps rather than adding more emails.

How many times should you follow up on a quote?

Two or three automatic messages is usually enough. After that, a short personal phone call works better than another email, and if there is still no answer, let it go politely.

What should you say in a quote follow-up email?

Name the job, check the quote arrived, offer to answer questions and give one easy way to reply. Keep it under 100 words, friendly and free of pressure.
