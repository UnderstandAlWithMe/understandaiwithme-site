Stop Chasing Timesheets Before Payroll: A Simple System for Small Teams

By Constantin · 2026-11-17 · 4 min read

For many small businesses, the slowest part of payroll is not the pay run. It is the two days before it: messaging staff for their hours, decoding handwritten sheets and fixing missed breaks. If you pay people by the hour, a simple timesheet system can save that time every pay period.

Why timesheets go wrong

Most timesheet problems come from the same few causes:

  • No fixed deadline. Hours arrive whenever people remember.
  • Too many formats. Paper, texts, emails and photos of whiteboards.
  • Filled in from memory. Hours written up at the end of the week are guesses.
  • No one checks before payroll. Mistakes are found after people have been paid.

Fix those four and most of the chasing disappears.

Step-by-step: set up the system

1. Pick one way to record hours

Choose one method and use it for everyone:

  • A time-tracking or rostering app where staff clock in and out on their phone or a shared tablet. Many connect directly to payroll software.
  • The time-tracking feature in your payroll or HR software, if it has one. This avoids re-typing hours.
  • A shared spreadsheet with one tab per person, if your team is very small. It works, but it relies on people filling it in.

An app is usually worth it once you have more than a handful of hourly staff, because it records start and finish times as they happen.

2. Decide what needs to be recorded

At minimum, record for each shift:

  • Date
  • Start and finish time
  • Unpaid breaks
  • Any overtime, and who approved it
  • Job, site or client, if you bill time to customers

3. Set one deadline and stick to it

For example: "Hours for the week must be submitted by 10am Monday." Put it in writing, add it to everyone's calendar and send an automatic reminder the afternoon before. Most time-tracking apps can send reminders for you.

4. Make one person responsible for approval

A manager or team lead reviews and approves hours before they go to payroll. Approving every day or two is easier than reviewing a whole week at once.

5. Run a five-minute check before payroll

Before each pay run, look for:

  1. Missing days or missing people
  2. Shifts with no break recorded
  3. Unusually long shifts or unapproved overtime
  4. Hours that do not match the roster

Fix these with the employee before you pay, not after.

Where AI helps

You can use ChatGPT or Claude to speed up the admin around timesheets:

  • Write the policy. Ask: "Write a one-page timesheet policy for a 12-person cafe. Cover how to record hours, breaks, overtime approval and the Monday 10am deadline. Plain, friendly language." Then edit it to fit your business and local rules.
  • Check a spreadsheet export. Upload a timesheet export (remove personal details you do not need) and ask: "Flag any shift over 10 hours, any shift with no break, and any day with no hours for someone on the roster. Show the results as a table." Always check the flagged lines yourself.
  • Draft the reminder message for your team chat or email.

Do not let AI calculate final pay. Use your payroll software for that.

Keep timesheet records

Timesheets are part of your payroll records, and employers have to keep records for set periods. A few examples from official sources:

  • US: the Department of Labor says records on which wage calculations are based, such as time cards, should be kept for two years, and payroll records for at least three years. See DOL Fact Sheet #21. State rules can add to this.
  • UK: HMRC says PAYE records must be kept for 3 years from the end of the tax year they relate to, with different rules for proving minimum wage and holiday pay. See GOV.UK: PAYE keeping records.
  • Australia: the Fair Work Ombudsman says employers have to keep time and wages records for 7 years. See Fair Work: record-keeping.

Elsewhere, check your local government business or employment site. A time-tracking app that stores records digitally makes this much easier than boxes of paper.

When to move to proper software

If you are still re-typing hours from one system into another, or spending more than an hour each pay period on timesheets, look at payroll and HR software with time tracking built in. Hours flow straight into the pay run, staff can see their own records, and the history is kept for you.

The short version

  • Use one method for recording hours, ideally an app.
  • Set one fixed deadline with an automatic reminder.
  • Have one person approve hours, little and often.
  • Check for gaps, missing breaks and overtime before every pay run.
  • Use AI for the policy, reminders and spotting odd lines, not for calculating pay.
  • Keep timesheet records for as long as your country requires.
