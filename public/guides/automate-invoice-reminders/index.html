How to Automate Invoice Reminders Without Chasing Anyone

By Constantin · 2026-10-24 · 3 min read

Late payment is rarely about customers refusing to pay. Most of the time the invoice went out late, got buried in an inbox, or nobody followed up. The fix is boring and very effective: send invoices the moment work finishes, and let software do the chasing.

This guide gives you a reminder schedule, three email templates, and the settings to switch on in almost any invoicing tool.

Step 1: Invoice the same day the work finishes

Every day between finishing a job and sending the invoice adds a day to when you get paid. Make invoicing part of finishing the job, not a Friday task.

  • Create invoice templates for your most common jobs so you only change the date and the customer.
  • If you use accounting software, turn on online payment links on invoices. People pay faster when paying is one click.
  • Put your payment terms on every invoice in plain words: "Payment due within 14 days".

Step 2: Set an automatic reminder schedule

Most accounting and invoicing tools can send reminders for you. Look for a setting called "invoice reminders" or "payment reminders". A schedule that works for most small businesses:

  1. 3 days before the due date: a friendly heads-up.
  2. On the due date: a short reminder with the payment link.
  3. 7 days overdue: a firmer note.
  4. 14 days overdue: a final reminder that says what happens next.

After that, a human should step in. Automation handles the forgetful customers; the genuinely difficult ones need a call.

Step 3: Use these three templates

Keep reminders short, polite and specific. Copy these and adjust the tone.

Before the due date

"Hi [Name], just a quick heads-up that invoice [number] for [amount] is due on [date]. You can pay online here: [link]. Thanks again for the work."

On the due date

"Hi [Name], invoice [number] for [amount] is due today. Here is the payment link again: [link]. If it has already been paid, please ignore this."

Overdue

"Hi [Name], invoice [number] for [amount] was due on [date] and is now [X] days overdue. Could you let me know when payment will be made? Pay online here: [link]."

Step 4: Use AI for the awkward ones

For customers who need a more personal message, an AI assistant such as ChatGPT or Claude can draft a reply in seconds. Give it the facts and the tone:

"Write a polite but firm email to a long-standing customer whose invoice is 21 days overdue. Mention that we value working with them and ask for a payment date this week. Keep it under 100 words."

Always read the draft before sending, and do not paste in bank details or anything you would not put in an email anyway.

Step 5: Look at the numbers once a month

Once a month, check two things: how many days on average it takes customers to pay, and which customers are always late. Consider shorter terms, deposits or upfront payment for repeat late payers.

A note on your rights

Many countries give businesses extra rights when commercial invoices are paid late. In the UK, you can claim statutory interest and compensation under late payment legislation (GOV.UK explains how). In the US and Australia the rules depend on your state and your contract terms, so put clear late-payment terms on your invoices and check your local government business site. You do not have to use these rights, but it helps to know they exist.

The short version

  • Invoice the day the work finishes.
  • Add online payment links.
  • Switch on automatic reminders at -3, 0, +7 and +14 days.
  • Step in personally after that.
