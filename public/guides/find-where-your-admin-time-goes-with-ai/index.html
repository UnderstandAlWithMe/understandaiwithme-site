Find Where Your Admin Time Goes: A One-Week Log Plus AI to Spot What to Fix First

By Constantin · 2026-11-10 · 4 min read

Most business owners know admin is eating their week, but not which part. Without a clear answer, it is easy to buy the wrong tool or automate something that only takes ten minutes a month.

This guide gives you a one-week time log, then shows you how to use ChatGPT, Claude or a similar assistant to turn it into a ranked list of what to fix first.

Step 1: Keep a rough log for one working week

A note on your phone, a spreadsheet or paper is fine. Every time you finish an admin task, write one line:

  • What you did: "replied to three quote requests", "matched bank payments to invoices".
  • Roughly how long it took, to the nearest five minutes.
  • What tool you used: email, spreadsheet, accounting software, phone.
  • Whether it is a repeat of something you already did this week.

If typing is a pain, record short voice notes as you go and turn them into text at the end of the day. Any transcription tool works; HappyScribe is one we list on our recommended tools page.

A rough log you actually keep beats a perfect one you abandon midweek.

Step 2: Tidy the log before you share it

Before pasting anything into an AI assistant, remove customer names, email addresses, amounts tied to named people and anything else private. "Chased payment from customer A" is enough detail for this exercise.

Also check your AI plan's data settings, so you know whether your conversations may be used to train models. Business and team plans often give you more control.

Step 3: Ask the AI to group and total your tasks

Paste your log into ChatGPT, Claude or a similar assistant with a prompt like this:

"Here is a one-week log of my admin tasks with rough times. Group similar tasks into categories. For each category, give the total time for the week, how many times it happened, and which tools were involved. Show the result as a table, sorted by total time, largest first. Do not invent any tasks or times that are not in the log."

That last sentence matters. AI assistants can fill gaps with guesses, so tell it to stick to your data. Then check the totals yourself with a quick sum, because AI can make arithmetic mistakes too.

Step 4: Ask what kind of fix each category needs

Next, ask the AI to sort each category into one of four buckets:

  1. Stop doing it. Some tasks exist out of habit, such as weekly reports nobody reads.
  2. Template it. Repeated emails, quotes and replies that follow the same pattern.
  3. Use a better tool or an AI workflow. Tasks that involve copying data between systems, chasing people or sorting documents.
  4. Hand it to someone. Tasks that need a person but not necessarily you.

A prompt that works well:

"For each category in the table, suggest which of these four buckets it fits: stop, template, tool or AI workflow, or delegate. Give one sentence of reasoning and one practical first step for each. Keep suggestions general and do not recommend specific paid products."

Treat the answers as a starting point for your own judgment. The AI only knows what is in the log.

Step 5: Pick one fix, not five

Look at the top two or three categories by time. Choose the one that is both time-consuming and easy to change. Ignore the rest for now.

Good first fixes are usually small:

  • Saving your five most common emails as templates.
  • Turning on automatic payment reminders in your invoicing software.
  • Taking a photo of each receipt at the moment of purchase instead of at month end.
  • Writing a one-page brief so a freelancer can take over a task.

Set a date two to four weeks from now to repeat the log for a few days. If the time on that category has dropped, move on to the next one.

Step 6: Put a rough value on the time

Hours are easier to act on when you see what they cost. Multiply your weekly admin hours by what an hour of your time is worth, then by the number of weeks you work in a year. Our free admin time calculator does this for you and shows which task to fix first.

That number helps you decide what is worth paying for. A task that costs hours a week may justify a tool or a build; one that costs twenty minutes probably needs only a template.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Logging only the big tasks. Small interruptions, like "finding that file again", often add up to more than the big jobs.
  • Automating a broken process. Simplify a confusing task before you hand it to software.
  • Changing everything at once. One fix per few weeks is easier to stick with and easier to measure.

The short version

  • Keep a rough admin log for one working week.
  • Remove private details, then ask an AI assistant to group and total the tasks.
  • Sort each category into stop, template, tool or delegate.
  • Fix the biggest easy win first, then measure again in a few weeks.
  • Check the AI's numbers yourself and keep the final decisions human.
