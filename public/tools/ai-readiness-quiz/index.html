Start with the basics

AI works best on tidy, repeatable work. Your quickest wins are simpler: get information out of paper and heads into one place, and pick one tool for money in and out. Then AI has something to work with.

Ready for quick wins

You have repeatable tasks and some systems in place. Pick one task you could write down step by step, and use AI or a better tool on that alone for a month. Expect hours back, not a revolution.

Ready to automate

Your processes are clear and your tools are connected. A small custom AI workflow, built around one high-volume task, is likely to pay back quickly. Start with a scoped pilot rather than a big project.

What “ready for AI” actually means

Being ready has less to do with technology than with how repeatable your work is. A task that follows the same steps every time, with information already in a digital tool, is a good candidate. A task that changes every time, or lives in someone’s head, needs a process before it needs AI.

A low score is useful too: it tells you that a simple tool or a checklist will save more time right now than any AI project.
